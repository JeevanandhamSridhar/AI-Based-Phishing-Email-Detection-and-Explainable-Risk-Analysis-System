# Research Gap Analysis and Literature Review

## 1. Academic Positioning and Defensible Framing

> **Defensible Research Framing (Verbatim):**  
> "In the literature reviewed for this project, we did not find a single locally-deployable, budget-constrained student system that combines multi-signal explainable phishing risk scoring with an explicit AI-authorship indicator and a self-directed adversarial evasion test. The individual techniques are established; the integration and the honest reporting of their limits is the contribution."

No claim of "first ever" is made anywhere in this project. The components—heuristic rule analysis, machine learning classifiers, SHAP/LIME explainability, stylometric text modeling, and adversarial testing—have all been explored individually or in enterprise-grade platforms. The research value of this capstone lies in the local-first synthesis of these components under strict resource constraints (Rs. 0 software cost, fully offline laptop execution) and the rigorous, transparent documentation of their limitations.

## 2. Review of Contemporary Literature (2024–2026)

The recent research landscape demonstrates that basic explainable phishing detection is already well-studied:

1. **Lim, B., Huerta, R., Sotelo, A., Quintela, A., Kumar, P. (2025).** *EXPLICATE: Enhancing Phishing Detection through Explainable AI and LLM-Powered Interpretability.* arXiv:2503.20796.  
   *Summary:* EXPLICATE pairs machine learning classifiers with explainable AI (SHAP and LIME) and utilizes large language model interpretability layers to render explanations into accessible language for end users. The authors evaluate their approach via interactive interfaces (GUI application and browser extension), achieving approximately 98% detection accuracy. This confirms that basic ML+XAI for phishing detection is already a solved baseline in current literature.

2. *MultiPhishGuard: An Explainable and Adaptive Multi-Agent LLM System for Phishing Email Detection.* (2025). arXiv:2505.23803.  
   *Summary:* Explores a multi-agent architecture utilizing large language models that cooperate to dissect email threats from multiple angles, emphasizing adaptive reasoning and explainable defense outputs.

3. *A Hybrid Heuristic-Machine Learning Framework for Phishing Detection.* (2025). Engineering, Technology & Applied Science Research, 15(5), 27219–27226.  
   *Summary:* Investigates a hybrid detection structure uniting static email authentication markers (SPF, DKIM, and DMARC verification), URL lexical inspection, and textual content analysis with machine learning algorithms, reaching greater than 95% accuracy. This establishes that combining multi-vector heuristics with ML is already established practice.

4. **Fatima, Z., Tanveer, M.H., Voicu, R.C., Chakravarty, S., et al. (2025).** *Explainable AI for IoT Devices and Robotic Communication Phishing Detection: A Machine Learning Approach Using LIME and SHAP.* ETASR, 15(5), 26478–26486.  
   *Summary:* Applies local and global attribution methods (LIME and SHAP) to machine learning phishing classifiers within constrained networked environments, highlighting how feature attribution demystifies black-box models.

5. **Opara, C., Modesti, P., Golightly, L. (2025).** *Evaluating spam filters and Stylometric Detection of AI-generated phishing emails.* Expert Systems with Applications, 276, 127044.  
   *Summary:* Investigates whether traditional spam filtering mechanisms and stylometric feature extractors can differentiate between human-written and LLM-generated phishing emails. By evaluating diverse stylometric markers across machine learning architectures, the authors achieve approximately 96% classification accuracy and a 0.99 ROC-AUC. This foundational study directly inspires our Module A stylometric feature set.

6. *Cross-model evaluation of phishing detectors against LLM-generated emails.* (2026). Frontiers in Big Data.  
   *Summary:* Evaluates how well classifiers trained on outputs from one specific LLM generalize when tested against emails generated by distinct, unseen LLM families. The study identifies significant cross-generator performance drops, establishing that in-distribution accuracy does not reliably transfer across different AI writing styles.

7. **Koide, T., Fukushi, N., Nakano, H., Chiba, D.** *ChatSpamDetector: Leveraging Large Language Models for Effective Phishing Email Detection.* arXiv:2402.18093 (2024); Springer LNCS version (2026).  
   *Summary:* Examines the utility of large language models in identifying sophisticated phishing and spam communications, demonstrating strong natural language understanding of deceptive context while discussing latency and computational overhead trade-offs.

8. **Denis, L., Meurant, C. (2024–25).** *Robustness Analysis of a Multi-Component Phishing Detection Model Against Explainable AI-Guided Adversarial Attacks.* MSc thesis, École polytechnique de Louvain.  
   *Summary:* Demonstrates that attackers who gain visibility into a phishing model's top explanatory features can systematically formulate adversarial rewrites to evade detection, achieving up to 100% evasion success in certain multi-component scenarios. This finding directly motivates our Module B self-directed adversarial evaluation.

9. *PiMRef: Deducing Ever-evolving Spear-phishing Emails with Knowledge Base Invariants.* arXiv:2507.15393.  
   *Summary:* Focuses on detecting targeted spear-phishing campaigns through structural knowledge-base invariants, addressing the limitations of static lexical triggers in the face of evolving adversary tactics.

10. *MeAJOR Corpus: A Multi-Source Dataset for Phishing Email Detection.* arXiv:2507.17978.  
    *Summary:* Compiles and standardizes multi-source phishing email corpora, emphasizing data diversity, header preservation, and benchmark consistency across evaluation protocols.

## 3. Identification of the Research Gap

From the survey of recent work, existing implementations fall into two main categories:
1. **Commercial / Cloud-Bound Platforms:** High computational cost, reliant on cloud API calls (e.g., proprietary LLM endpoints), presenting privacy concerns and recurring financial overhead.
2. **Standard Academic Prototypes:** Typical final-year implementations stop after training a single classifier (e.g., TF-IDF or BERT) and displaying token-level SHAP/LIME charts. They do not evaluate whether the phishing email was synthetic/LLM-generated, nor do they test whether their own explainability outputs can be exploited by an adversary to evade the system.

### The Two First-Class Contributions of this Project:

### Gap 1: AI-Generated / LLM-Authored Phishing Identification (Module A)
- **Problem:** With generative AI widely accessible, adversaries increasingly use LLMs to generate grammatically fluent, persuasion-optimized phishing emails that lack traditional spam markers.
- **Our Implementation:** Following Opara et al. (2025), we construct a secondary stylometric classifier examining lexical diversity (Type-Token Ratio), sentence length variance, function word distribution, punctuation ratios, and readability indices.
- **Realistic Reporting:** Drawing from the 2026 Frontiers in Big Data findings, our evaluation does not merely report optimistic in-generator accuracy; we explicitly evaluate cross-generator performance (training on LLM A and B, testing on LLM C) and document generalization drops transparently in `docs/ai-authorship-eval.md`.

### Gap 2: Explanation-Guided Adversarial Self-Evaluation (Module B)
- **Problem:** Explainable AI is typically presented as a pure defensive advantage. However, as Denis & Meurant (2024–25) revealed, XAI feature attributions provide adversaries with a roadmap for evasion.
- **Our Implementation:** We implement an automated adversarial evaluation module. The system extracts its own top positive XAI predictive triggers (e.g., "urgent", "verify account", "immediate action") and evaluates the pipeline's robustness against hand-crafted semantic rewrites that replace these triggers while retaining malicious intent.
- **Honest Limitations:** Instances where paraphrased emails successfully evade detection or cause risk scores to drop from High to Low are documented as system limitations rather than concealed.
